Week 1

Introduction & XR Landscape

Key Definitions

  • VR (Virtual Reality) – fully synthetic digital environment; replaces real world entirely
  • AR (Augmented Reality) – overlays digital content onto the real world
  • MR (Mixed Reality) – real and virtual objects interact in real time
  • XR (Extended Reality) – umbrella term for VR, AR, MR

Hardware Evolution

  • Oculus Rift DK1 (2013) → first consumer-grade VR HMD
  • Meta Quest 2/3 – standalone, no PC required
  • Apple Vision Pro (2024) – spatial computing, eye+hand+voice input
  • Microsoft HoloLens – enterprise AR headset
  • Snap Spectacles – AR glasses with Lens Studio SDK

Course Learning Outcomes

  1. Explain hardware and software components in immersive applications
  2. Design and implement software components for immersive systems
  3. Evaluate key aspects of immersive user experiences

Week 2

Foundational Immersion

Reality-Virtuality (RV) Continuum

Proposed by Milgram & Kishino (1994). A spectrum from real to virtual environments with Mixed Reality in between.

Real ←────────────────────────────────────→ Virtual
Real Env │ Augmented Reality │ Augmented Virtuality │ Virtual Env

Four Dimensions of the RV Continuum

DimensionFull FormDescription
EWKExtent of World KnowledgeHow much the system knows about the real world
RFReproduction FidelityHow realistically the environment is reproduced
EPMExtent of Presence MetaphorDegree to which user feels present/immersed
COCharacter Orientation (Fixity)Whether virtual characters face/track the user

Presence Illusions

Place Illusion (PI) – The illusion of being in the virtual environment; "being there". Driven by perceptual realism and tracking accuracy.

Plausibility Illusion (Psi) – The illusion that what is happening in the VE is real; believability of events. Driven by narrative and interaction design.

Key: PI = where, Psi = what is happening

Types of Presence

  • Spatial Presence – sense of being physically located in the VE
  • Involvement – degree of attention and engagement
  • Realism – how realistic the environment feels
  • Social Presence – sense of being with other people/agents

Week 3

Evaluating Immersive Experiences

Immersion vs Presence vs Flow

  • Immersion – objective system properties (hardware/software capabilities)
  • Presence – subjective user experience of "being there"
  • Flow – optimal engagement state (Csikszentmihalyi)

Flow – 8 Dimensions

  1. Challenge-skill balance
  2. Clear goals
  3. Unambiguous feedback
  4. Action-awareness merging
  5. Sense of control
  6. Loss of self-consciousness
  7. Transformation of time
  8. Autotelic experience (intrinsic reward)

Cybersickness – 3 Clusters

  • Nausea – stomach awareness, nausea, retching
  • Oculomotor – eyestrain, difficulty focusing, headache
  • Disorientation – dizziness, vertigo, fullness of head

Cause: Sensory conflict theory – mismatch between visual motion and vestibular system

Evaluation Instruments

InstrumentMeasuresType
IPQPresence (Spatial, Involvement, Realism)Quantitative
SSQSimulator Sickness (N, O, D)Quantitative
VRSQVR-specific sicknessQuantitative
Flow ScaleFlow dimensionsQuantitative
Think-aloudUsability, thoughts in real-timeQualitative
InterviewsIn-depth user perspectivesQualitative
PhysiologicalHeart rate, EDA, eye-trackingMixed

Immersion as System Properties

  • Display resolution
  • Field of View (FOV)
  • Refresh rate (Hz)
  • Tracking accuracy (6-DOF)
  • Rendering fidelity
  • Latency (motion-to-photon)

Week 6

Immersive Systems II: HMD Optics

Thin Lens Equation

1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i

f = focal length | d_o = object distance | d_i = image distance
Magnification m = -d_i / d_o

Key HMD Parameters

ParameterTypical RangeEffect
IPD (Inter-Pupillary Distance)58–72 mmStereo depth perception; incorrect IPD → eye strain, incorrect depth
Eye Relief10–20 mmDistance from lens to eye; affects FOV and glasses accommodation
FOV (diagonal)90–110°Wider = more immersive; limited by display size and focal length
Focal LengthVariableDetermines magnification and virtual image distance

Vergence-Accommodation Conflict (VAC)

Vergence – eyes rotate inward/outward to converge on object depth (changes with object distance)

Accommodation – eye lens adjusts focus (in VR, fixed at focal length ~2m)

Problem: Eyes converge at different distances but always focus at fixed VR focal plane → visual fatigue, headaches

Solutions being explored: Light field displays, varifocal displays, eye-tracking guided rendering

Lens Distortion

  • Barrel distortion – image bulges outward (caused by convex VR lenses)
  • Pincushion distortion – image pinches inward
  • Pre-correction: render pincushion-distorted image → lens converts back to straight

Stereo Rendering

  • Two separate render passes – one per eye
  • Each eye has slightly different view matrix (IPD offset)
  • Each eye has asymmetric off-axis projection (due to IPD)
  • Foveated rendering – high res at gaze point, low res periphery (eye tracking required)
  • Multiview rendering – GPU renders both eyes in one pass (WebGL extension)

View Frustum in VR

Due to IPD offset, left and right eye frustums are asymmetric – not centered on display. Off-axis projection matrix must account for this. The overlap region of both frustums is what the user "sees" in stereo.

HMD Simulator Deep Dive

Simulator Architecture (6 Modules)

  • main.ts – entry point, bootstraps app
  • app.ts – Babylon.js engine and scene setup
  • hmd.ts – HMD parameter calculations (optics)
  • ui.ts – user interface controls
  • constants.ts – default HMD parameters
  • frustumVisualizer.ts – renders view frustum visualization

Barrel Distortion Shader

Google Cardboard V2 coefficients: k1 = 0.34, k2 = 0.55

// GLSL barrel distortion formula
r² = x² + y²
distortion = 1 + k1·r² + k2·r⁴
x' = x · distortion
y' = y · distortion

Off-Axis Projection Matrix

Unlike standard symmetric perspective projection, VR uses off-axis projection per eye:

  • Left eye: frustum shifted right (half IPD offset)
  • Right eye: frustum shifted left (half IPD offset)
  • Results in correct stereo parallax for depth perception

Key Relationships

Parameter ChangeEffect
↑ IPDMore depth separation; wider frustum divergence; deeper/shallower perceived depth
↑ Eye ReliefWider effective FOV; less comfortable for glasses wearers
↑ Focal LengthMore magnification; virtual image appears further away
↑ Lens-to-display gapAffects object distance d_o → changes magnification

Interaction & Virtual Environment Creation

VE Creation Approaches

Model-BasedImage-Based (360°)
DOFFull 6-DOF3-DOF (rotational only)
InteractivityHigh (objects manipulable)Low (passive)
EffortHigh (modelling + texturing + lighting)Low (360° camera capture)
RealismStylized or realistic (artist dependent)Photorealistic
Use casesGames, simulations, trainingVirtual tours, empathy experiences

Interaction Authenticity Spectrum

  • Natural – 1:1 mapping to real-world action; e.g., physical walking, direct hand grasping
  • Augmented Natural – real action + virtual enhancement; e.g., gravity gloves (Half-Life: Alyx), walk-in-place locomotion
  • Artificial (Magical) – purely virtual mechanics with no real-world equivalent; e.g., teleportation, time manipulation

Interaction Agency

  • Implicit (Passive) – triggered by system; e.g., auto-facing NPC, triggered events
  • Explicit (Active) – deliberate user action; e.g., pressing button, grabbing object

Five Locomotion Methods

MethodSickness RiskPresenceNotes
TeleportationNone/LowBreaks immersionMost comfortable; disrupts flow
Joystick ContinuousHighHighNatural feel but strong vestibular conflict
IMU Walk-in-PlaceLowHighAuthentic; needs extra hardware (IMU sensors)
Real WalkingNoneHighestMost natural; limited to room scale
360° TreadmillNoneHighLarge spaces; expensive and bulky

Embodiment

Three factors enabling embodiment:

  1. Visual appearance – does avatar look like me?
  2. Visuomotor synchrony – does avatar move when I move?
  3. First-person perspective – viewing from avatar's eyes

Cybersickness Mitigation

  • Vignette effects (reduce peripheral vision during movement)
  • Stable visual references (e.g., cockpit/nose in view)
  • User control over locomotion speed
  • Teleportation instead of continuous movement
  • Reduce latency (motion-to-photon)
  • Maintain target frame rate (72+ FPS)

IPA 2 · Feature Contribution · HMD Simulator

HMD Simulator – Lens Distortion & Chromatic Aberration

Edwin's contribution to DIAversity · Live: hmd.diaversity.org · Babylon.js 8.26.1

What Edwin Implemented

  1. Barrel Distortion Sliders (k1, k2) – real-time GLSL shader uniform updates
  2. No-Distortion Toggle – saves/restores k1/k2 values; instant comparison
  3. Chromatic Aberration Simulation – per-channel R/G/B distortion offsets in shader
  4. Grid Overlay – dual implementation: shader-based procedural + 3D wireframe meshes at lens position
  5. Parameter Labels – descriptive text below each slider

Distortion Shader Logic

Radial polynomial distortion applied in GLSL post-process shader:

r² = x² + y²
distortion = 1 + k1·r² + k2·r⁴
x' = x · distortion
y' = y · distortion
  • k1 – primary barrel distortion coefficient (r², dominates near centre)
  • k2 – fine-tunes edges (r⁴, affects periphery)
  • Google Cardboard V2 defaults: k1 = 0.34, k2 = 0.55

Chromatic Aberration

Different wavelengths (R, G, B) refract at slightly different angles through a real lens. Simulated by sampling each colour channel at a slightly offset UV coordinate:

  • R channel – largest offset (longest wavelength)
  • G channel – medium offset
  • B channel – smallest offset (shortest wavelength)
  • Result: colour fringing visible at lens periphery
  • Slider controls aberration strength (0.0–1.0 uniform)

Rendering Architecture

  1. Main camera renders full scene (scene + HMD + frustum layers)
  2. Left eye camera → RenderTargetTexture → viewport 20–45% of canvas
  3. Right eye camera → RenderTargetTexture → mirrored viewport
  4. PostProcess applies distortion + chromatic aberration per eye
  5. Grid overlay toggles procedurally via shader showGrid uniform

Layer Masking System

LAYER_SCENE = 0x1 // primitives
LAYER_HMD = 0x2 // HMD hardware mesh
LAYER_FRUSTUM = 0x4 // frustum wireframes
LAYER_UI = 0x8 // GUI overlay
LAYER_SPLAT_MAIN = 0x10 // main camera Gaussian splat
LAYER_SPLAT_LEFT = 0x20 // left eye splat
LAYER_SPLAT_RIGHT = 0x40 // right eye splat

Each camera's layerMask is set so it only renders the meshes it should see — prevents eye cameras seeing HMD hardware mesh, etc.

Rec Room: Quest 3 vs PC – XR User Study

Edwin's role: Flow Subteam Data Analyst · Team: irwenfanclub

Research Question

How does platform (Quest 3 VR vs PC desktop) influence users' spatial presence, social presence, flow, and comfort/cybersickness during Rec Room activities?

Study Design

  • Design: Within-subject (each participant used both platforms)
  • n: 10 participants, all male, age 25
  • Order: PC → Quest 3 (counterbalanced)
  • Task: 10 different Rec Room activities (Paintball, Bowling, Escape Room, Charades, Disc Golf, etc.)
  • Duration: ~12–15 min per platform
  • Data collection: Post-task Likert surveys + observer notes

Constructs & Instruments

ConstructScaleItems
Spatial Presence1–7 Likert6 items (sense of being there, realism, physical location, interaction naturalness, space awareness, surrounded)
Flow1–7 Likert6 items from FSS + FKS + GameFlow
Comfort (overall)1–7 single item"How comfortable was your experience?"
Cybersickness Symptoms0–3 per symptom5 symptoms: Nausea, Dizziness, Eye Strain, Headache, Fatigue

Flow Scale Items (Edwin's Construct)

ItemFlow DimensionSource
"I was completely focused"ConcentrationFSS
"I felt in control"Sense of controlFKS
"I always knew what to do next"Clear goalsGameFlow
"I received clear feedback"Unambiguous feedbackFSS
"Difficulty matched my ability"Challenge-skill balanceFKS
"I lost track of time"Temporal distortionFSS

Key Findings

ConstructQuest 3PCDifferenceVerdict
Spatial Presence5.62 (SD 0.59)5.13 (SD 0.51)+0.49 VRVR advantage
Flow5.22 (SD 0.43)5.10 (SD 0.56)+0.12 VRNegligible difference
Comfort (overall)5.70 (SD 1.06)5.50 (SD 1.27)+0.20 VRNegligible
Symptom Sum (0–15)2.40 (SD 2.12)1.10 (SD 1.37)+1.30 VRVR = 2× symptoms

Symptom Breakdown

  • Eye Strain: Quest 3 1.00 vs PC 0.40 (2.5× higher in VR) – visual tasks (aiming, scanning)
  • Nausea: Quest 3 0.70 vs PC 0.20 (3.5× higher) – free-roaming activities
  • Dizziness: Both ~0.10–0.20 – Rec Room's comfort locomotion effective
  • Fatigue: Quest 3 0.40 vs PC 0.00 – HMD weight and thermal load
  • 70% Quest 3 users reported any discomfort vs 50% PC users

Key Interpretations

  • High comfort ratings despite higher symptoms → participants distinguished enjoyment from physical discomfort
  • Flow was equal on both platforms → flow can be achieved through good UX regardless of hardware
  • Activity type mattered more than platform for some constructs (social tasks ≈ same; action tasks → VR advantage)
  • Rec Room's conservative locomotion design effectively prevented dizziness
